## 4. Current official U.S. source review (not a legal opinion)

### 4.1 FTC Act §45
15 U.S.C. §45 is an identified federal baseline against unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce. CE's practical design implication is to keep statements about Credits, price, covered reading scope, purchase limit, delivery status, unknown/pending states and remedies accurate and non-misleading. This broad statute does not itself decide CE's entire transaction state machine or every refund outcome. Official source: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A15+section%3A45+edition%3Aprelim%29

### 4.2 FTC Cooling-Off Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 429
The FTC describes this rule as addressing certain off-premises/home/door-to-door sales and related cancellation disclosures. Do not infer a universal three-business-day cancellation right for ordinary online purchases from this rule. That does not rule out other federal, state, contractual, card-network or transaction-specific rights. Official FTC source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/cooling-period-sales-made-home-or-other-locations

### 4.3 FTC Mail/Internet/Telephone Order Merchandise Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 435
FTC guidance states that the merchandise order rule does not cover services. Do not copy its merchandise shipping-delay algorithm as the complete CE digital-service refund/fulfillment policy. This does not imply CE has no other consumer obligations. Official source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule

### 4.4 Regulation E §1005.20 — Credits classification remains unresolved
The CFPB's current rule and official commentary say coverage depends on whether the relevant card/code/device meets the definitions for gift certificate, store gift card or general-use prepaid card and whether an exclusion applies. Official commentary explains that items redeemable for a specific service generally are not covered when not issued in a specified/denominated amount, but an instrument issued in a specified or denominated amount can be covered; reloadability and whether it is marketed as a gift card also matter. See 12 C.F.R. §1005.20 and its Official Interpretation: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/ and https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/interp-20/

CE currently has an illustrative 4 Credits = US$2.99 configuration, account-owned non-expiring Credits, top-ups and redemption for CE services. The inspected sources do not settle whether the actual proposed Credits instrument represents a specified/denominated monetary amount, how the balance/code is issued, whether transfer/gifting is permitted/marketed, whether any exception applies, or the effect of the final payment arrangement. **Do not claim either that Regulation E applies or that it is excluded.** Qualified review should use the exact selected product and transaction facts. State stored-value/gift-certificate/unclaimed-property rules may also matter, depending on those facts and the paid-market scope.

## 6. Recommendation

**Keep paid checkout closed until a specific selected transaction configuration and paid-market scope have a documented readiness disposition. Continue all non-checkout CE development and candidate preparation meanwhile.**

Before making a readiness claim, require a single source-backed packet to identify and reconcile:
1. seller/legal entity and relevant nexus; processor or merchant-of-record structure and contract; actual payment methods;
2. final SKU/package sizes, Credits denomination/price/currency/tax/mandatory fees, redemption and top-up/reload, transfer/gifting/resale/cash-out policy;
3. final preview/terms, covered reading scope, order confirmation and status disclosures;
4. Credits classification and applicable federal/state/territorial consumer/remedy requirements for the selected paid market;
5. precise order, reservation, debit, unknown state, fulfilment, failure, exact-once restoration, monetary refund, replacement and daily-slot state machine;
6. identified authoritative commerce persistence source and independently verified transaction/ledger/fulfillment behavior with the applicable test oracle;
7. a controlled jurisdiction enablement record plus separate source/runtime/deployment authorization.

This list is a readiness recommendation, not a claim that every item must be answered by outside counsel. The questions genuinely requiring qualified legal analysis should be isolated and not allowed to halt all unrelated CE engineering.
